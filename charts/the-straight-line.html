The Straight Line

The share of American adults holding a bachelor’s degree has risen about half a percentage point a year for nearly fifty years. Six recessions, nine presidents, the personal computer, the internet, offshoring, smartphones, and a pandemic all happened along this line. None of them bent it.

Method: Percent of the civilian population age 25 and over with a bachelor’s degree or higher, from the Census Bureau’s Current Population Survey as published in NCES Digest Table 104.10. Values for 1975 and 1980 are the published benchmark years; the series is annual from 1985. The dashed reference is a straight line drawn between the 1975 and 2023 endpoints, not a statistical fit, so the eye can judge how little the data departs from it. Shaded columns are NBER-dated recessions.
